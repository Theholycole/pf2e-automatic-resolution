# Rule-family index and Phase 2–3 batch queue

The broad families below are the reusable implementation units. Close-wording cohorts split their source examples into manageable review sets. Singletons are retained when descriptions have no sufficiently close match; they can still benefit from the broader family primitive. None of these cohorts authorizes a new spell-name branch.

## What the phases mean

These are implementation capabilities from the [full implementation plan](../../docs/planning/full-spell-implementation-plan.md), not exclusive categories of spell rules. A spell can need several phases.

| Phase | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | GM corrections: review and adjust provisional targets, save degrees, condition values and durations before finalization. |
| 2 | Outcome application: apply the finalized save outcome—native damage, conditions and Effects—while preserving each cast’s contributions, eligibility, damage totals and duplicate prevention. |
| 3 | Ongoing management: maintain consequences after application—turn/world-time expiry, recurring saves, condition recovery, Sustain, dismissal, aura/exposure review and selective cleanup through reloads or GM changes. |
| 4 | Immediate workflows: spell attacks, healing, no-roll or mixed resolution, casting choices, and explicit object/location targets. |
| 5 | Transformations: choose legal forms, apply form mechanics and granted actions, track limits, and safely revert. |
| 6 | Summons and created creatures: select eligible creatures, place and control them, track cast ownership and clean up the correct creations. |
| 7 | Spatial effects: walls, objects, areas, movement and teleportation, including geometry, durability and scene placement where reliable. |
| 8 | Rituals: take the confirmed ceremony result and resolve its immediate and delayed outcomes, saves and persistent records. |
| 9 | Catalog completion: resolve remaining mechanical gaps and classification questions, and verify every in-scope spell and meaningful variant. |

**Phase 2 versus Phase 3:** creating a timed Clumsy effect or a persistent-damage condition belongs to Phase 2; expiring that effect or handling later damage/recovery belongs to Phase 3. The same spell can use both. Native PF2e may supply part of either workflow.

The **primary worklist** assigns one place to organize implementation; cross-phase signals record other needed capabilities. Phase 1 corrections and Phase 9 acceptance apply across the catalog. The 51 entries assigned primarily to Phase 9 are the deferred classification/review list, not the entire scope of Phase 9.

## Suggested first review batches

Prioritize Phase 2–3 groups with unenabled members and an already enabled route as a regression starting point, followed by additional ongoing primitives. These are candidate priorities, not estimates of implementation effort. The enabled example is not automatically a verified live test anchor. Read all pending mechanics before reusing evidence.


## Per-spell differences that must remain in tests

- Condition value versus affliction stage; initial damage versus persistent damage.
- Primary versus secondary saves, omitted degree inheritance, and optional choices.
- Caster versus recipient anchors; current versus next turn; start versus end.
- Exact linked contribution versus any matching condition on the actor.
- Native Effect availability versus complete mechanical automation.
- Every legal rank/action variant, recipient predicate, immunity and scope restriction.

Current classification deliberately leaves uncertain mechanics for source review. Unknown clauses must remain gated while a recognized clause is generalized.
